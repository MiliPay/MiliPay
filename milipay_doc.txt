1. Executive Summary
MiliPay is a biometric payment ecosystem, currently at seed stage, designed to let customers in Nigeria make secure, cardless payments directly from their linked bank accounts using fingerprint (with palm print and passcode fallback) authentication at the point of sale. MiliPay is explicitly not a digital wallet or a custodian of customer funds — it is an authentication and payment-initiation layer that sits between the customer, the merchant's POS terminal, and the customer's existing bank account.
The venture's long-term ambition is to become Nigeria's leading biometric payment network, with a planned Phase 2 expansion across Africa. The current prototype consists of a React Native customer mobile application backed by Supabase, with a connected database, and is under active development by a small founding team covering product vision, frontend, backend, and cybersecurity/operations.
This document consolidates the company overview, product design, technical architecture, regulatory posture, business model, risks, and roadmap into a single reference suitable for investor conversations, engineering onboarding, and early discussions with prospective banking partners and regulators. Where information has not yet been finalized by the founding team, this is explicitly flagged as an assumption or an open question rather than invented.

3. Problem Statement
Physical payment cards remain the dominant non-cash payment method for point-of-sale transactions in Nigeria, but they carry well-documented weaknesses: cards can be forgotten, lost, stolen, cloned via skimming, or physically damaged, and each of these failure modes creates friction, fraud exposure, or a failed transaction at the point of sale. Customers without their card on hand cannot pay, and card-present fraud (skimming, cloning) remains a persistent issue across card-based payment networks.
For merchants, card failures and fraud disputes create operational friction and chargeback risk. For banks, card issuance, replacement, and fraud monitoring are recurring costs. A payment method that authenticates the person rather than a physical token addresses the root cause of these problems rather than mitigating symptoms.
4. Proposed Solution
MiliPay proposes a biometric payment network in which a customer's fingerprint (primary), palm print (secondary/backup), or secure passcode (fallback) authenticates a payment instruction that is sent directly to the customer's linked bank account — with MiliPay never custodying customer funds. Customers register once, in person, through authorized MiliPay agents who capture identity documents (NIN) and biometric data under controlled conditions. At the point of sale, the merchant's biometric POS device captures a live fingerprint, matches it against the enrolled template, and — on a successful match — the customer's bank is instructed to debit the selected linked account.
This removes the physical card from the transaction entirely while keeping funds custody with regulated deposit-taking banks, which is likely to be a materially easier regulatory conversation than a wallet or stored-value model.

6. Mission Statement
To build a secure, fast, and trusted cardless payment ecosystem that removes physical payment cards as a point of failure for everyday transactions.
7. Core Values
Security first — biometric and financial data are held to the highest achievable standard, not the minimum legal bar.
Trust through transparency — customers, merchants, banks, and regulators should always be able to understand what MiliPay does and does not do with their data and funds.
Speed without shortcuts — 'milliseconds' refers to the authentication experience, not to skipped verification or compliance steps.
Inclusion — biometric payment should be accessible to customers who are unbanked-by-card but banked-by-account, and to merchants of varying sizes.
Partnership over disintermediation — MiliPay positions itself as a layer that strengthens banks' existing relationships with customers, not one that competes with or replaces them.

13. Product Overview
MiliPay consists of four principal components:
Customer mobile application (React Native) — account linking, default account selection, enrollment status, transaction history.
Agent registration application/dashboard — full enrollment workflow including identity capture and biometric capture.
Certified biometric POS devices — merchant-facing hardware for live fingerprint/palm capture and match-based transaction authorization.
Backend platform (currently Supabase) — enrollment data storage, biometric template matching orchestration, and payment-instruction routing to partner banks.
MiliPay explicitly does not hold customer funds; every successful authentication results in an instruction routed to the customer's own linked bank account, not a MiliPay-held balance.

15. Registration Workflow
Registration is agent-mediated rather than fully self-service, which is a deliberate control point for identity assurance.
Customer approaches or is approached by an authorized MiliPay agent (a MiliPay employee, not a third-party contractor under the current brief).
Agent captures: full name, email address, phone number, NIN (National Identification Number).
Agent captures biometric data: two left-hand fingerprints, two right-hand fingerprints, and a palm print (held as backup authentication).
Agent submits enrollment through the agent registration application/dashboard.
Backend stores enrollment data and biometric templates, and the customer's profile becomes active for account linking.
Customer links one or more bank accounts, designating a default account for payments.
Panel Note — Cybersecurity Consultant & Banking/Payment Infrastructure Expert perspective
NIN capture should be verified against NIMC's records (not merely recorded as entered) to prevent enrollment fraud — please confirm whether NIN verification via NIMC's API is part of the current or planned build.
Biometric templates should never be stored as raw images if avoidable; store irreversible templates/hashes where the matching algorithm supports it, encrypted at rest, with strict access controls and audit logging on every read.
Recommend a documented agent vetting and background-check process, since agents are the single most sensitive trust point in the entire system — they have access to customers' identity and biometric data.
Open question: what happens if a customer needs to re-enroll (e.g., fingerprint damage, disputed enrollment, or agent misconduct)? A revocation/re-enrollment procedure should be defined before pilot.
16. Payment Workflow
Customer shops at a MiliPay-enabled merchant.
Merchant selects MiliPay as the payment method on the POS device.
Customer selects which linked bank account to debit, or uses their default account.
Customer authenticates via fingerprint.
If fingerprint authentication fails: palm print is attempted; if that fails, a secure passcode is used as final fallback.
On successful authentication, a payment instruction is sent to debit the selected account at the customer's bank.
MiliPay does not custody funds at any point in this flow — funds move from the customer's bank account toward settlement with the merchant.
17. System Overview
At a high level, the system consists of four cooperating surfaces around a central backend:
Customer mobile app (React Native) ↔ Backend (Supabase) — enrollment status, account linking, transaction history.
Agent app/dashboard ↔ Backend — enrollment submission, biometric capture upload.
Merchant biometric POS device ↔ Backend — live authentication requests and match results.
Backend ↔ Partner bank(s) / payment switch — payment-instruction routing after successful authentication.
The prototype currently implements the first two surfaces; the POS-device integration and the bank/switch integration are the two components with the largest remaining technical and partnership uncertainty.

Supabase is a reasonable choice for early-stage velocity, but for a system handling biometric templates and payment instructions, plan an explicit migration or hardening path before scale: dedicated encryption-at-rest key management, network isolation for the matching engine, and a clear boundary so that a Supabase misconfiguration cannot expose biometric data (this is a common real-world failure mode for early fintech backends). The brief describes the business model as provisional. The founding team is evaluating several potential revenue streams:
Merchant transaction fees — a per-transaction or percentage fee charged to merchants, similar to card-scheme interchange models.
Strategic bank partnerships — potential revenue-share or integration-fee arrangements with partner banks.

Panel Note — VC Investor & FinTech Consultant perspective
Investors will want to see at least a working hypothesis with rough magnitudes even at seed stage — e.g., 'we believe a X% merchant fee is viable based on comparable card-present interchange rates in Nigeria' — rather than four options with no lean. Recommend the team pick a primary hypothesis to lead with, while keeping the others as backup options, before the next investor conversation.
Also recommend clarifying who bears the cost of POS hardware initially (MiliPay capex, merchant purchase, or bank-subsidized) since this materially affects both the balance sheet and the merchant-acquisition sales motion.
20. Market Analysis
Nigeria has a large and growing digital payments market, with strong existing rails (NIBSS Instant Payment, bank transfer apps, and USSD) alongside continued heavy reliance on physical cards at POS. A biometric authentication layer competes less with existing payment rails and more with the physical card as the authentication/authorization mechanism at the point of sale — a narrower and more specific wedge than 'payments' broadly.
21. Competitive Advantage
Not a wallet — by routing directly to existing bank accounts rather than holding stored value, MiliPay avoids competing with banks for deposits and likely faces a lighter regulatory burden than an e-money/wallet license would require (to be confirmed with counsel and CBN).
Agent-mediated enrollment — a controlled, in-person identity-assurance step that is harder for pure app-based competitors to replicate quickly at the same trust level.
Multi-factor biometric fallback (fingerprint → palm print → passcode) — reduces failed-transaction rate relative to single-modality biometric systems.
First-mover potential in a biometric-specific POS category in Nigeria, if no direct competitor currently operates at this stage (this should be verified with current competitive research before being asserted to investors).

31. Conclusion
MiliPay's core insight — authenticate the person, not a physical card, while leaving funds custody with regulated banks — is a defensible and regulator-legible position for a Nigerian fintech at seed stage. The most urgent open items are: finalizing the settlement-rail decision with a banking or switch partner, resolving the business model to a single leading hypothesis, hardening the security architecture around biometric data before any real customer enrollment, and closing the small but real gaps in team coverage (compliance). None of these are unusual for a seed-stage company, but they are the items most likely to come up in the first substantive investor and bank conversations, and addressing them proactively — rather than reactively — will materially strengthen MiliPay's position.
